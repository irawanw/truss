- If TRUSS loops and llama-paw on the same pack does not, it is an engine bug (H2/H5): fix it first and stop weight

## 4. The weight design: pack "S1-X31" (1×3090, 64 GB RAM)

### 4a. Principles

1. **One format per expert, the same bytes on GPU and CPU** (Strata's rule).
   - Every expert lives once in a pinned RAM arena.
   - Resident experts are copies of those bytes in VRAM.
   - A miss is computed by the CPU in place, or copied over PCIe.
   - Weights never depend on cache history.
2. **No hole.** Every expert's error ≤ ½ of Strata's on that expert: K ≥ 2 trellis, so no K1 and no K1.5. This
   directly targets H1.
3. **Rates come from measurement.** Per-expert error is measured from X3.1 encodes at several K. Usage comes from a
   **traffic mix that includes the user's agent sessions**, not chatcode alone.
4. ~~A trellis expert costs the CPU per weight, not per byte.~~ **Corrected by E4 (#107): in the decode shape the
   22-thread CPU tier is RAM-bound (22-28 GB/s), so a cold expert costs its bytes, on the CPU as over PCIe.** Cold K
   is not free; every miss is charged its bytes (residency report, #108).
5. **X3.1** = the x3up encoder (exllamav3 v1.5.1: scale refit, LDLQ drift) plus **fractional K** (1.5, 2.5, 3.5).
   - On PAW-27B the encoder alone tied X3. The gain came from fractional rates at equal bytes: K3.5 gave KL 0.0211
     vs 0.0239 (`paw27b/20260923_x3up/reports/x3up_handover_20260924.md`).
   - So expect the gain here mostly from the allocation (floor, traffic, fractional steps), not from the encoder.

## 5. The engine: light, every resource busy

### 5a. Decode pass budget for 140 tok/s

Sampled acceptance today is 0.49-0.60 per draft with ≤ 3 drafts, which gives *(est.)* τ ≈ 2.3-2.6 tokens/pass.
140 tok/s at τ 2.5 needs **≤ 17.9 ms/pass**. **Target 15 ms, budget 17.9.**

| per pass, 4-5 row window, 256K | now (bench, 34.8 ms) | target | how |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| dense / non-MoE (PLE, hc, mixer, router, shared, combine) | ~14.5 | **7.0** | X3.1 dense (4.7 → 2.8 GB read; floor 3.5-4.0 ms at 700-800 GB/s); one CUDA graph per window size; fused layer kernels (≤ 600 launches, from 2,672) |
| GPU expert hits | 4.5 | **3.3** | ~1,150 unique hits × ~1.86 MB = 2.1 GB at ≥ 650 GB/s; more experts per block at 4-5 rows (old D3) |
| CPU misses (exposed wait) | 13.2 | **1.5** | E4 per-expert cost 1.22 → 0.6 ms; work starts from pre-gate hints one layer early; runs under GPU time |
| PCIe copy waits | ~1-3 | **0.3** | copies only for hinted misses, on their own stream, one layer ahead (§5b) |
| head + sampling + drafts | ~2.5 | **2.2** | head K5 (0.4 GB); MTP steps in the same graph |
| host gaps | ~0.9 | **0.3** | host does only sample, stop check, next window |
| **pass** | **34.8** | **~14.6** | τ 2.5 → **171 tok/s**; τ 2.3 → 157 |

The 3.3 ms margin covers renter load and longer contexts.

### 5b. Can the miss path absorb ~310 misses per pass? *(est.)*

- **PCIe.** 13.5 GB/s × 15 ms = 200 MB per pass, about **100 experts at K3**. Pre-gating predicts 72% of the next
  layer's experts (`docs/reference/model-qwen4exp.md`, FFN residency). Hinted misses are copied one layer ahead on a **separate low-priority stream**: #92 showed
  that sharing the demand stream cost +5.6 ms.
- **CPU.**
  - The remaining ~210 misses × 0.6 ms / 22 threads = **5.7 ms** of pool time, spread over 48 layers: ~0.12 ms per
    layer, against a ~0.30 ms GPU layer. That is hidden if the pool starts on hints.
  - RAM traffic is 210 × 1.86 MB = 390 MB per 15 ms = **26 GB/s**, half of the 54-57 GB/s measured.
- **Requirement:** in-engine CPU cost ≤ 0.6 ms of thread time per expert (today 1.22; unit test 0.62; Strata 0.84).
  **This is the critical engine item.**
  - If it stays above 0.8 ms, the pack moves toward C1 (more resident experts, lower K), or the cold experts switch
    to a CPU-cheap codebook (E4c), and the solver re-runs.

### 5c. Prefill budget for 4,000 tok/s (21.7K prompt at n_ctx 256K)

- **FLOPs** *(est.)*:
  - dense 3.46 B params + 10 active experts 2.36 B = **11.6 GFLOP/token** of GEMM;
  - DSA, indexer and GDN add ~1-1.5 GFLOP/token at 128-256K;
  - total **≈ 13 GFLOP/token**.
- **At 4,000 tok/s that is 52 TFLOP/s** sustained.
  - With GEMMs at ≥ 150 TOPS int8 mma (a CUTLASS-class kernel on a 3090; today's `q8_gemm` measures 72), GEMMs take
    ~0.31 s per second of prefill. The non-GEMM work gets ~0.6 s.
  - P0 measures that split before anything is built.
- **Chunk 16,384** (today 8,192). With C2, 45.8 GB pack − 16.9 GB resident = 28.9 GB is cold.
  - Streaming it at 13.5 GB/s takes **2.14 s per chunk**: 3,800 tok/s at 8K chunks, so it would cap us below the
    target.
  - At 16K chunks it is 2.14 s against 4.10 s of budget, overlapped with compute.
- **Three-way expert split per layer** (all resources):
  - resident experts on the GPU;
  - cold experts with ≥ T* tokens streamed over PCIe into borrowed VRAM (double buffer);
  - cold experts with < T* tokens computed by the CPU in place (many rows per expert amortize the trellis decode;
    22 cores × 32 MAC/cycle × 3.5 GHz ≈ 2.5 TMAC/s *(est.)*).
  - T* is fitted from P0's histogram of tokens per cold expert.
- **Short fresh tails** (agent turns add 1-8K new tokens): cold data movement bounds them.
  - A 2K tail still touches nearly every expert: 2K × 10 / 512 ≈ 40 tokens per expert.
  - With the three-way split *(est.)* ~0.8 s → **~2,500 tok/s**. That is the secondary gate; 4,000 is not physical
    there.
- **Gates:**
  - 21.7K prompt ≥ 4,000 tok/s;
  - 2-8K tails ≥ 2,500 tok/s;
  - served fresh-read median ≥ 3,000 tok/s.

| E5 | **Three-channel miss path:** hinted misses over PCIe one layer ahead on a separate low-priority stream; unhinted misses on the CPU; the per-layer split from the measured cost model (#75) | PCIe copy wait ≤ 0.3 ms/pass |
| E6 | **Paged KV:** KV pages allocated as the context grows; the expert cache uses the free tail and gives pages back by evicting its lowest-ranked experts | resident experts at 120K ≥ at 256K + 1,000; needles 5/5 to 262K |
| E7 | **Prefill pipeline, beyond Strata's.** Strata's fused prompt-expert kernel (v0.1.36: int8 activations once per token, (token, k) grouped by expert on the GPU with no host sync, ≤ 128 experts per launch, experts read in place, a 1024-slot ring; 11.12 → 3.13 ms/layer at 2,048 tokens) is the **floor to beat, not the design to copy**: it unpacks 2-bit codes, which are nearly free, and leaves the CPU idle during prefill. Ours: **(a) decode once per CTA** — a persistent CTA owns one expert, decodes each trellis weight tile to int8 in shared memory once and runs every token tile of that expert over it (trellis decode costs ~10x a 2-bit unpack, so per-tile re-decode would lose); **(b) gate/up → SwiGLU → down in one kernel** per 64-row tile, H kept in shared memory (Strata writes H out and launches down separately); **(c) three-way split** — resident on the GPU, cold with ≥ T* tokens streamed over PCIe one layer ahead, cold with < T* tokens on the 22-core pool at the same time (Strata streams every cold expert); **(d) 16K chunks by default** (Strata: 8K, 32K opt-in), the ring sized by the B4 solver; (e) PLE rows for the next chunk prefetched under the current one | §5c gates; per-layer routed time at 2,048 tokens ≤ Strata's 3.13 ms despite the trellis decode; fused vs unfused rel RMS ≤ 1e-4 |
| E8 | **Drafting, beyond Strata's.** MTP re-encoded (§4d). Strata's **coupled draft sampling** (`STRATA_SPEC_COUPLED`: under sampling the MTP head samples with the target's chain, penalties included, and the same Philox uniform the verify row draws; exact-match verify keeps the text unchanged) is the starting point; it is opt-in there with no published acceptance numbers. Ours adds: **(a) draft length per request from running acceptance** (1-5; Strata fixes `--spec`); **(b) a two-branch first position** — when the coupled draft's probability is < 0.5, also verify the runner-up in the same window (5 rows, the same cost bucket as 4 on our window kernel), so a near-tie costs no round; **(c) prompt-lookup drafts** for code edits and quoted text as Strata does, gated on measured acceptance | sampled τ ≥ 2.5 on agent replays (Strata's greedy level); output identical with drafting on/off for a fixed seed |
| E9 | **Light host:** RSS ≤ 52 GB; no per-token Python work beyond SSE (detokenize and `ToolStream` stay off the engine thread); one engine thread plus the pool | peak RSS logged per run |

### 12b. Budget for 120 tok/s on C2 *(est.)*

120 tok/s at 3.1 tokens/pass = 25.8 ms per pass. Rest of pass after E1/E2 ~15-17 ms (Strata's non-expert GPU time is
~17.7 ms, #77) → **≤ ~9 ms for misses**. C2 at 16.9 GB leaves ~235 misses/pass (#108, x1.16); with CPU (16.6/ms) and
PCIe (7/ms) fully parallel that is ~10 ms: over budget. So 120 needs, in this order of size:
1. **E1/E2** (one graph per window; today's rest of pass is 22 ms with 2,672 launches): pack-independent, first.
2. **Fewer misses at C2**: an expert profile from agent traffic (R3, held-out test), admission that never delays a
   demand fetch (copy-kernel reads or idle-link scheduling), E6 at < 190K.
3. **CPU cost per cold expert** (R4): the tier is ALU-bound, so a CPU-cheaper codec for cold experts pays directly.
   Candidates, all gated on error at ≤ C2's per-expert error budget: (a) v2one-style codebook on AVX2 (one `vpmulld`
   per 16 weights instead of the 16-bit hash; GPU numbers #37: 1.5-1.7x, +10-15% error at K2-2.5), extended to S = 6;
   (b) a LUT/`pshufb` scalar-VQ format at ~3.25 bpw for cold experts only (more bytes, fine: RAM has headroom);
   (c) the solver may then keep cold experts in the CPU format and resident ones in trellis (two formats, each
   expert stored once; prefill streams the CPU format to the GPU, so (a)/(b) need a GPU decoder too).
4. **MTP** (R5): tokens/pass 3.1 → 3.5 moves the budget to 29 ms. Inputs: MTP experts re-encoded with a real Hessian
   (§4d), acceptance per position at long context, draft length per window.

### 12c. Prefill 4,000 tok/s on C2 *(est.)*

### 12g. Re-ordered after #111-#113 (2026-10-03)

- **150K pass (served config, renters at load ~20): GPU compute 19.5 ms + idle 2.7 + waits on misses ~25** (CPU join
  13-21 ms, swings with renter load; copies 6-7). Per layer the CPU part is the critical path.
- **R4 (CPU codec) drops in value under the chosen pack:** replay of 12k@K3.5 + K2.5 at 16.9 GB puts **72% of the
  non-resident routings on K3.5 experts** (7.3k of them do not fit in VRAM and carry most of the mass). v2one stops at
  K2.5 (S <= 5) and costs +15% error there (#37); the mul1 CPU kernel is ~10% from its Zen 2 floor (#96). A K3.5
  CPU-cheap codec would be new research; not now.
- **Admission on the idle link** (#113): ~3% per token at 150K, off by default (one unexplained hang).
- **Next: E6, the ring borrows the unused KV tail.** The int8 K/V caches are sized for 262,144 positions (~3.4 GB for
  13 DSA layers incl. MTP); an agent turn at 100-190K leaves 1-2.4 GB of it unused. The ring gets those tails as extra
  segments (FIFO across segments; an entry never straddles one); before KV rows are written, the positions ahead are
  reclaimed (entries there evicted, the compute stream waits for the copy stream). Replay: +1.1 / +3 GB of cache =
  -10-14% / -25-30% misses. Gate: CPU tier off, tokens identical with E6 on/off; then 150K A/B served config.
- Then E1/E2 (gaps 2.7 ms, ~2 ms of small kernels), then the dense X3.1 encode (+1.8 GB VRAM) in Phase B.
